**Subject: Request for academic feedback on a preliminary study of YOLO11n-based helminth egg detection in faecal-smear microscopy images**

Dear Professor / Research Team,

I am writing to share a preliminary computational research study entitled **“YOLO11n-Based Detection of Four Helminth Egg Categories in Faecal-Smear Microscopy Images”** and to ask whether an appropriate researcher or research group at the University of Oxford might be willing to consider providing academic feedback.

The study evaluates a lightweight YOLO11n object-detection model for four helminth egg categories: *Ascaris lumbricoides*, hookworm, *Schistosoma mansoni*, and *Trichuris trichiura*. It uses a selected subset of 400 image–annotation pairs from the publicly available Soil-transmitted Helminths and *Schistosoma mansoni* Eggs Image Dataset (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.13843815), partitioned into 280 training, 60 validation, and 60 test images. The documented test evaluation reports precision of 77.56%, recall of 63.89%, mAP@0.50 of 73.48%, and mAP@0.50–0.95 of 55.84%.

I want to emphasise that this is an early-stage computational study, not a clinically validated diagnostic system. The manuscript discloses important limitations, including the small selected sample, the absence of established patient- or slide-level independence, the lack of independent biological verification of the source annotations, exploratory reuse of the test subset under alternative settings, an unresolved training-configuration discrepancy, and the absence of external validation.

I would be grateful if you could advise whether the work may be relevant to a research group working in parasitology, infectious disease, digital microscopy, medical image analysis, or applied machine learning. Any academic comments on the study design, evaluation protocol, interpretation, or possible directions for strengthening the research would be valuable. If this enquiry is outside your area, I would appreciate guidance toward a more suitable academic contact, if possible.

The research materials are being shared for academic consideration without requesting payment, funding, or any publication commitment. I understand that the University of Oxford is not a journal submission platform; this letter is an academic enquiry and not a claim that the University has agreed to review, endorse, publish, or validate the work.
